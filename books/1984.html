FILE // D-001

Nineteen Eighty-Four

George Orwell, 1949
Primary mechanism: control of reality itself

The Warning

Orwell wrote 1984 not as a prediction but as an extrapolation — he took the propaganda machinery, surveillance methods, and language control he'd already watched at work under Stalin and the wartime British state, and asked what happens if a government perfects them completely. The result is a world where the state doesn't just punish thoughtcrime; it makes wrong thoughts structurally difficult to think at all.

The System

Oceania runs on three interlocking mechanisms: telescreens that watch and listen everywhere, Newspeak that shrinks the vocabulary available for dissent, and the Ministry of Truth's constant rewriting of the past. None of the three works alone — surveillance catches deviation, language prevents it from forming, and historical revision erases the evidence that things were ever different.

What It Got Right

The telescreen's core insight — a device in every home that watches as easily as it entertains — anticipated always-on cameras and microphones in ordinary consumer electronics by seven decades. The idea that historical records could be quietly and continuously revised, leaving no trace of the original, reads very differently in an era of edited web pages and deleted posts than it did in 1949.

What It Got Wrong

Orwell's surveillance state is centralized, visible, and explicitly coercive — Big Brother wants you to know you're watched. Real-world surveillance, when it has expanded, has mostly done the opposite: distributed across companies rather than one ministry, largely invisible, and often volunteered rather than imposed. The book underestimated how much control could be achieved through convenience rather than fear.

What Exists Today

Facial recognition in public spaces, persistent data retention by governments and corporations, and coordinated content removal all have real analogues to Oceania's tools — though dispersed across many actors rather than one state. See Surveillance Notes for ongoing, dated examples.
